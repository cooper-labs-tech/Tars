## 3. You are responsible for what your agents do

Tars starts AI agents that act on your behalf, with your user account's permissions. Depending on the settings you choose, they can:

- run shell commands, and read, change or delete files;
- use the network;
- post messages and publish content through services you connect;
- spend money on paid APIs.

Some permission modes, which you can select, let agents act without asking you first. A template you import can set such a mode, folders and a first prompt: Tars shows them before saving it, and using a template sends its prompt only when you turn that on. AI models make mistakes and can be steered by the content they read.

You are responsible for:

- choosing which agents run, in which folders, with which permission mode;
- reviewing what they produce before you rely on it, merge it, publish it or run it in production;
- keeping backups of anything that matters.

Stopping or deleting an agent, or quitting Tars, ends the tasks it was delegated and the commands they started. Work an agent finished before that, such as files it changed or messages it sent, is not undone.
